# Problem
A drunkard begins at a vertex of a cube and at each step moves to one of the 3 adjacent vertices with equal probability.  After 3 steps, what is the probability the drunkard is back at the starting vertex?

answers = $\frac{1}{27}$, $\frac{2}{27}$, $\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{4}{27}$, $\frac{5}{27}$


# Solution

Model the cube's vertices by 3-bit coordinates:

\[
000,\ 001,\ 010,\ 011,\ 100,\ 101,\ 110,\ 111.
\]

Two vertices are adjacent exactly when they differ in one bit, so each move flips exactly one bit.

The starting vertex can be taken as \(000\), whose bit-sum is even.  
After each move, the parity of the bit-sum switches (even \(\leftrightarrow\) odd). Therefore:

- after 1 step: odd parity,
- after 2 steps: even parity,
- after 3 steps: odd parity.

But the starting vertex \(000\) has even parity, so it is impossible to be back there after an odd number of steps.

\[
\boxed{0}
\]

So the probability is \(0\).
